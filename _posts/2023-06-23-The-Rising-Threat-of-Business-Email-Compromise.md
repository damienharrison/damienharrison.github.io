In recent years, electronic mail (or email, as it's more commonly known) has become an indispensable part of our everyday lives. We use it for a variety of purposes, including business transactions. However, with our growing reliance on digital technology, we've also seen a rise in cybercrime, and one of the most significant threats facing businesses today is Business Email Compromise (BEC).

![Image of a suspicious email warning](/images/email-scam-warning.jpg)

So why should we pay particular attention to BEC attacks? Simply put, they're on the rise. BEC attacks saw an astonishing 81% increase in 2022, and a worrying 98% of employees fail to report the threat.

## What is Business Email Compromise (BEC)?

BEC is a type of scam wherein criminals employ email fraud to target victims. These victims can be both businesses and individuals, particularly those who carry out wire transfer payments.

Scammers posing as high-level executives or business partners send emails to employees, customers, or vendors, requesting them to make payments or transfer funds in some form.

According to the FBI, BEC scams cost businesses an alarming $1.8 billion in 2020, a figure that rose to $2.4 billion in 2021. These scams can inflict severe financial damage on businesses and individuals, not to mention the harm they can do to their reputations.

## How Does BEC Work?

![Image of a hacker](/images/hacker.jpg)

BEC attacks are often highly sophisticated and carefully crafted, making them difficult to identify. The attacker first conducts research on the target organisation and its employees, gaining insight into the company's operations, suppliers, customers, and business partners.

Much of this information is freely available online. Scammers can find it on sites like LinkedIn, Facebook, and companies' websites. Once the attacker has enough information, they can craft a convincing email designed to appear as though it comes from a high-level executive or a business partner.

The email typically requests the recipient to make a payment or transfer funds, often under the guise of an urgent and confidential matter. The email often creates a sense of urgency, compelling the recipient to act quickly. The attacker might also use social engineering tactics, such as posing as a trusted contact or creating a fake website that mimics the company's site, to make the email seem more legitimate.

If the recipient falls for the scam and makes the payment, the attacker makes off with the funds, leaving the victim with financial losses.

## How to Fight Business Email Compromise

![Image of cybersecurity](/images/cybersecurity.jpg)

BEC scams can be tricky to prevent, but there are measures businesses and individuals can take to reduce the risk of falling victim to them.

### Educate Employees

Organisations should educate their employees about the risks of BEC, including providing training on how to identify and avoid these scams. Training should also cover email account security, such as checking their sent folder regularly for any strange messages, using strong email passwords, changing their email password regularly, storing their email password securely, and notifying an IT contact if they suspect a phishing email.

### Enable Email Authentication

Organisations should implement email authentication protocols, including Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting, and Conformance (DMARC), Sender Policy Framework (SPF), and DomainKeys Identified Mail (DKIM). These protocols help verify the authenticity of the sender's email address and reduce the risk of email spoofing.

### Deploy a Payment Verification Process

Organisations should deploy payment verification processes, such as two-factor authentication and confirmation of payment requests through a separate communication channel. This can help prevent unauthorized transactions.

### Regularly Update and Patch Systems

Ensure your systems are always up to date. Cybercriminals often exploit vulnerabilities in outdated systems, so regular updates and patches can help keep your systems secure.

## In Conclusion

Business Email Compromise is a growing threat that businesses and individuals must take seriously. While the threat is real, taking preventative measures and raising awareness can significantly reduce the risk. Stay vigilant, stay educated, and most importantly, stay safe.
